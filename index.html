Quick grammar guide

CAN / CAN’T

Ability, possibility and informal permission. Can + base verb; never can to.

I can swim. / She can’t drive. / Can I sit here?

MUST / MUSTN’T

Must = strong obligation, often the speaker’s opinion. Mustn’t = it is forbidden.

You must be on time. / You mustn’t cheat.

HAVE TO / DON’T HAVE TO

Have to = obligation often coming from a rule or another person. Don’t have to = not necessary. Third person: has to / doesn’t have to.

We have to wear badges. / He doesn’t have to work on Sunday.

SHOULD / SHOULDN’T

Advice and recommendations. Use should + base verb.

You should sleep more. / You shouldn’t skip breakfast.

MIGHT / MAY

Possible but uncertain events in the present or future. Might often sounds a little less certain, but the difference is not fixed.

It might rain. / She may be late.

COULD / COULDN’T

Past general ability, polite requests and possibility.

I could read at six. / Could you help me? / It could happen.

MAY NOT / MIGHT NOT

Something possibly will not happen. May not can also express formal prohibition, so context matters.

She might not come. / It may not be warm tomorrow.

PERMISSION & REQUESTS

Can I…? everyday permission; Could I…? more polite; May I…? formal. Could you…? polite request.

Could I use your pen? / May I come in? / Could you open the door?
Important distinctions: mustn’t = forbidden, but don’t have to = optional. Must and have to often overlap, but must typically highlights the speaker’s personal insistence whereas have to often refers to external requirements.
Form: modal + base verb (she can swim, not she can swims). No do/does to form a question with can, must, should, could, may or might; have to is different (Does she have to go?).
